# Pricing record checks

Checked 6 November 2026. These are limited record checks for an operator to review, not legal certification or a substitute for checking booking channels and terms.

## Australia: total displayed price

[ACCC price displays](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/pricing/price-displays) requires the total displayed price to include taxes and unavoidable or preselected fees. The property records whether its room rates already include taxes and when compulsory fees were reviewed. The proposal adds its fixed compulsory nightly fee. Unconfirmed tax basis or an old fee review blocks approval.

This base covers one room-night with a fixed nightly fee. Per-person, per-stay, conditional, card and optional charges need a custom calculation and channel-specific display review. It does not calculate GST or prove that an advertised price complies. Use tax-inclusive input amounts and review the full booking total and conditions before applying prices.

## New Zealand: accurate accommodation offers

[Commerce Commission leisure and travel guidance](https://www.comcom.govt.nz/consumers/dealing-with-typical-situations/leisure-and-travel/) explains that accommodation descriptions, prices and availability should be accurate when a booking is made. The model retains source rates, dated occupancy and rate approvals, and refuses approval from missing, stale, unavailable or sold-out evidence. It does not inspect the public booking page or change an existing booking price.

## Internal checks

The two-day occupancy limit and 90-day fee review window are business policies, not statutory periods. The CLI prints the official source for each jurisdiction and a separate internal-policy result. REVIEW RECORDED means that a review date exists; it is not a compliance pass. The pricing-evidence check highlights stale records. OTHER jurisdictions require their own rules before operational use.

No guest identities are needed. Review source exports for personal information before import. This tool retains approved rate decisions and notes, but a database owner can modify records directly, so the log is not tamper-proof. Access controls, retention, backups and privacy procedures remain operational setup work.
